title: "The People Who Automated Themselves"
date: 2026-07-24
---

Developers are often described as one of the professions most exposed to AI. But there is a more interesting way to look at it.

Developers may be among the first people to **build the tools that make their own work less necessary**.

There is a natural feedback loop in technology. Developers build software, software makes development more efficient, and developers use those gains to build even more software.

AI makes that loop tighter.

The interesting part is not whether developers disappear. It is that the people closest to the technology are also the most capable of seeing where work can be removed, and the most likely to use the tools that remove it.

In that sense, developers are not simply being automated. They are participating in their own automation.

This is why coding may change faster than many other professions. The important shift is not that engineers disappear, but that the economic value of **manually producing code** may fall faster than the need for engineering itself.

A developer who once spent a week implementing a feature may eventually spend an hour defining the problem, setting the constraints, reviewing the architecture, and directing agents that handle the implementation.

The work moves up the stack.

And the pattern extends beyond software. Developers are building the technology layer that makes knowledge work more efficient. As that layer becomes more capable, the same economics can reach QA, support, documentation, analysis, operations, finance, HR, legal, and sales.

The sequence matters less than the direction.

**The people who build the automation are creating the conditions for everyone else to be automated too.**

That is the strange position developers now occupy.

We are not just using AI to make ourselves more productive.

We are building the machinery that makes productivity itself increasingly cheap.

And once that machinery works across enough of the economy, the interesting question is no longer whether AI will replace particular jobs.

It is what happens when **the people who built the automation are no longer special because everyone has it.**
